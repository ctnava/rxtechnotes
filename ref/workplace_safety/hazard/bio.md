# 🦠 Handling Biohazards & Infection Control

## Definitions

### Nosocomial Infections

Nosocomial (hospital‑acquired) infections are illnesses transmitted within healthcare settings, typically between **patients**, **staff**, and **visitors**. These infections are commonly caused by **bacteria, viruses, or fungi** and frequently present as:

- Bloodstream infections  
- Pneumonia  
- Urinary tract infections  
- Surgical site infections  

> 🩺 *Hospitalized patients often have weakened immune systems, making infection prevention critical.*

### 🛡️ Infection Control Committees  

Most hospitals maintain an **Infection Control Committee (ICC)** responsible for:

- Surveillance of nosocomial infections  
- Developing policies & procedures  
- Providing employee education during onboarding  
- Conducting audits and compliance checks  
- Assigning annual competencies related to infection prevention  

The committee is multidisciplinary, typically including nurses, pharmacists, physicians, microbiologists, and environmental services.

## 🧤 Standard (Contact) Precautions  

Standard precautions apply to **all patients**, regardless of diagnosis. They are designed to prevent exposure to:

- Blood  
- Bodily fluids  
- Mucous membranes  
- Non‑intact skin  
- Contaminated surfaces  

Healthcare workers must use appropriate **PPE** and avoid direct contact with infectious materials.

If exposure occurs:

- Perform **immediate handwashing**  
- Report the incident to a supervisor

### ✋ Hand Hygiene  

Handwashing is the **single most effective method** of preventing nosocomial infections.

Healthcare workers must wash hands:

- After patient contact  
- After touching bodily fluids or contaminated surfaces  
- Before donning gloves  
- After removing gloves  

> 📌 *Hands must be washed even if gloves were worn.*

🔗 [Aseptic Handwashing Protocol](../handwashing/readme.md)

### 🥼 Basic Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

PPE includes:  

- Gloves  
- Gowns  
- Goggles  
- Face shields  

These barriers protect against **splashes** and **short‑term exposure**, but prolonged use can turn PPE into a contamination risk. PPE must be changed frequently and removed using proper technique.

## Advanced Precautions

### 🌬️ Airborne & Droplet Precautions  

Some pathogens spread through **respiratory droplets** or **airborne particles**, such as tuberculosis (TB). Additional precautions include:

- Surgical masks (droplet)  
- N95 respirators or PAPRs (airborne)  
- Negative‑pressure rooms for airborne diseases

### 🧬 Immunological Precautions

Common immunizations for healthcare workers include:  

- Hepatitis B: Due to close contact with bodily fluid
- MMR  
- Influenza  
- COVID‑19  

> 🐻 *Most California hospitals require proof of vaccination before employment.*

HIV Exposure Protocols:

- **Post‑Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP)** — taken after potential HIV exposure; time‑sensitive and less effective, but standard in healthcare
- **Pre‑Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)** — taken preventively; widely covered by insurance for people who have engage in sexual intercourse with males who have sex with other males

## Biohazard Disposal

**Biohazard disposal** refers to the safe segregation, containment, and removal of materials contaminated with *blood, bodily fluids, infectious agents, or sharps*. These items pose a significant risk of **infection transmission**, making proper disposal essential for protecting healthcare workers, patients, and the environment. Because biohazardous waste can spread disease through **sharps injuries**, **surface contamination**, or **accidental splashes**, facilities must follow strict, legally regulated procedures that align with **OSHA**, **CDC**, and state public‑health standards.

Proper biohazard disposal ensures that infectious materials are handled using **approved containers**, **color‑coded waste streams**, and **controlled removal processes**, reducing the risk of exposure and maintaining a safe clinical environment.
