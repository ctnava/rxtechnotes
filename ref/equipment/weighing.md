# Weighing Equipment ⚖️

## Balances

Balances determine **mass** by measuring **weight**. Proper use and maintenance ensure accuracy and prevent cross‑contamination.

### Balance Maintenance 🛡️

`Must be placed where it won't be moved again during the day to maintain calibration`

To maintain accuracy and extend equipment life:

- Place the balance on a **stable, level, vibration‑free surface**  
- Ensure the area is **draft‑free**; avoid air vents, fans, or open windows  
- **Level the balance** by centering the leveling bubble using adjustable feet  
- At the **start of each day**, perform internal calibration or verification  
- Clean spills immediately using a **lint‑free towelette**  
- Turn off the balance at the end of the day  
- Never place materials directly on the weighing pan. Use paper or boats
- Keep **draft shields/glass doors closed** when measuring and storing
- **Clean the balance & arrest the pans** (lock mechanism) before moving or storing  

> 📌 **Tip:** Even small vibrations (footsteps, carts, HVAC) can affect readings on sensitive balances.

#### Calibration Weights

Calibration weights are **standardized, certified masses** used to verify and adjust balance accuracy.

- Typically range from **1 g to 50 g**, plus fractional weights **10–500 mg**  
- Stored in a **protective box** to prevent contamination  
- Always handled with **forceps** to avoid oils, corrosion, and residue

### Class III Torsion Balances (Prescription Balances)

`For quantities >120 mg and <60 g (sometimes up to 120 g)`

A **two‑pan mechanical balance** using internal and external weights.

### Features

- Uses a **rider** to add internal weights on the right pan  
- Measures in **metric** (primarily) and sometimes **apothecary** units  
- Minimum weighable quantity: **120 mg**  
- Typical error: **5%**  
- Sensitivity: **6 mg** (amount needed to move pointer one division)

> 📌 Still found in many community pharmacies, though electronic balances are now preferred.

### Sensitivity Workaround (Aliquot Method)

When a prescription requires **less than 120 mg**, technicians must:

1. Create a **homogenized mixture** (active + inert diluent)  
2. Weigh a **larger, measurable amount**  
3. Dispense the correct fraction of the mixture

This ensures accuracy despite the balance’s sensitivity limitations.

### Electronic Balances

Modern pharmacies primarily use **electronic (digital) balances** due to higher accuracy and ease of use.

**Features**:

- Digital display  
- Internal calibration functions  
- Faster, more accurate than torsion balances  
- Available as **top‑loading** or **enclosed** models

#### Analytical Balances

`For quantities <120 mg`

Used for **high‑precision** weighing in milligrams or micrograms.

- Sensitivity typically **±1 mg** or better  
- Often enclosed to prevent air disturbance  
- Common in **research labs**, less common in retail pharmacy

> 🚨 **Do not use analytical balances for bulk powders or sticky substances**. They are extremely sensitive and easily damaged.

## Weighing Papers & Boats

Used to protect the balance pan and prevent contamination or drug loss.

### Best Practices 🛡️

`Eliminating the need for repeated washing and reducing drug loss to porous surfaces.`

- Always use **weighing paper or disposable boats**  
- Use a **clean paper/boat for each ingredient**  
- Avoid porous or absorbent surfaces  
- Use a **spatula** to add or remove ingredients  
- Fold weighing paper to create a **controlled pour**  
- Never touch weighing paper with wet or gloved hands (static + contamination risk)

> 📌 Boats are preferred for **granular or sticky** ingredients; paper is ideal for powders.
