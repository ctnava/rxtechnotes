# Mixing Solids & Semi‑Solids

Compounding often requires **reducing particle size**, **creating uniform mixtures**, and **incorporating solids into semi‑solid bases**. Proper equipment and technique ensure accuracy, stability, and patient

## Equipment

### Mortar & Pestle

Commonly used for **mixing, blending, and particle‑size reduction** of solids and semi‑solids.

**Materials:**

- **Wedgewood**  
  - Porous, coarse surface  
  - Best for **crystals, granules, and hard powders**  
  - Excellent for **trituration**  
  - Not suitable for staining or oily compounds

- **Porcelain**  
  - Porous, Smoother than wedgewood  
  - Good for **soft powders** and **semi‑solids**  
  - Still provides mild grinding action

- **Glass**  
  - Smooth, non‑porous  
  - Best for **liquids**, **creams**, **ointments**, and **colored or oily substances**  
  - Not ideal for particle‑size reduction

### Sieves

Used to ensure powders are **fine, uniform, and non‑irritating**.

**Definition:**  
A sieve is a mesh screen that separates particles based on size.

**Mesh Number:**  
The number of **openings per linear inch**.

- **#40 mesh** → 40 openings per inch  
- Higher mesh number = **smaller openings** = **finer powder**

**Common Mesh Sizes & Uses**:

| Mesh Size | Particle Size | Common Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| #20 | Coarse | Granules, initial screening |
| #40 | Moderate fine | Most compounded powders |
| #60 | Fine | Dusting powders, dermatologic powders |
| #80–100 | Very fine | High‑precision powders, potent APIs |

> 📌 Sieving is typically performed **after trituration** to ensure uniformity.

### Spatulas

Used to **transfer**, **mix**, and **manipulate** solid and semi‑solid ingredients.

**Types:**

- **Stainless steel**  
  - Strong, durable  
  - Best for most powders  
  - ⚠️ Can react with iodine, mercury, and some acids

- **Hard rubber or plastic**  
  - Non‑reactive  
  - Preferred for **iodine**, **mercury compounds**, and **acidic ingredients**

**Best Practices:**

- Always clean **before and after** use  
- Use **separate spatulas** for incompatible ingredients  
- Use a **flexible spatula** for creams/ointments on an ointment slab

## Techniques

### Trituration

`Particle Size Reduction`

The process of **grinding a solid into a fine powder** using a **Wedgewood or Porcelain** (ideal) mortar and pestle.

This increases **surface area**, improves **uniformity**, and enhances **mixing**
the process of grinding is called **trituration**

### Geometric Dilution

`Ensures even distribution of potent or low‑dose ingredients.`

Used when mixing powders of **unequal quantities** to ensure a **homogeneous blend**.

**Process:**

1. Place the **smaller quantity** of powder in the mortar.  
2. Add an **equal amount** of the larger‑quantity powder.  
3. Triturate thoroughly.  
4. Add another portion of the larger powder equal to the mixture in the mortar.  
5. Repeat until **all powders** are incorporated.

### Spatulation

`Purely Mixing; No Trituration`

A gentle mixing technique using a spatula on an **ointment slab**, **pill tile**, or **plastic bag**.

**Characteristics:**

- No pressure applied  
- No particle‑size reduction  
- Produces a **light, fluffy mixture**  
- Ideal for powders that **should not be triturated**

Primary Use: **Eutectic Mixtures**

Some powders **liquefy when mixed together** due to melting point depression.  

To prevent liquefaction:

1. Triturate each eutectic ingredient **separately** with an inert carrier powder  
   (e.g., magnesium oxide, calcium carbonate, starch, lactose)  
2. Combine the diluted powders by **spatulation**, not trituration

**Common Eutectic Substances:**

- Camphor  
- Menthol  
- Salicylic acid  
- Benzocaine  
- Lidocaine  
- Phenol  
- Thymol

> 📌 Spatulation prevents heat and pressure that would otherwise cause eutectic mixtures to liquefy.
