# Solutions

---

## 🔑 Definitions

### Solution

A **solution** is a *homogeneous, clear* liquid preparation in which a **solute is completely dissolved** in a **solvent**.  

> 📌 *Clear does not mean colorless.*

### Solvent

The **solvent** is the liquid that dissolves the solute.  
In pharmacy compounding, the solvent is often called the **vehicle**.

### Solute

The **solute** is the substance being dissolved; commonly the **drug** or active ingredient.

---

## 🔑 Physical Principles

### Solubility

Solubility describes how much solute dissolves in a given amount of solvent.

| Term | Parts of Solvent per 1 Part Solute |
| --- | --- |
| Very soluble | < 1 |
| Freely soluble | 1–10 |
| Soluble | 10–30 |
| Sparingly soluble | 30–100 |
| Slightly soluble | 100–1,000 |
| Very slightly soluble | 1,000–10,000 |
| Practically insoluble | > 10,000 |

📌 **Like dissolves like:**

- Polar solutes dissolve in polar solvents (e.g., water).  
- Nonpolar solutes dissolve in nonpolar solvents (e.g., oils, alcohols).

### Saturation

**Saturation** occurs when a solvent has dissolved the **maximum amount of solute** at a given temperature.  
Any additional solute will remain **undissolved**.

🛡️ *Heat increases solubility for most solids, but not all.*

### Volume Contraction

`Mass is conserved; volume is not.`

When two liquids are mixed, the **final volume may be less than the sum of the individual volumes**.

This is called **volume contraction**.

#### Why It Happens

- Molecules of different liquids **fit into each other’s empty spaces**  
- Strong intermolecular forces (e.g., hydrogen bonding) pull molecules closer  
- Common when mixing:
  - **Aqueous + alcoholic solutions**  
  - **Aqueous + glycerin/propylene glycol**  
  - **Aqueous + concentrated electrolytes**  
  - **Aqueous + nonaqueous solvents**

#### Practical Implications 🛡️

- **Never add volumes** to estimate final volume  
- Always **QS** to final volume in a **TC** device  
- When compounding:
  - Dissolve solids first  
  - Add liquids in correct order  
  - QS at the end  

#### Example

Mixing **50 mL ethanol + 50 mL water**  
Final volume ≈ **96–97 mL**

> 📌 **Key Rule:** Always QS to final volume.

## 🛡️ General Preparation Principles

- Weigh solids using a **balance**  
- Most solids dissolve easily; some require **trituration** to increase surface area  
- Heat may be used to increase dissolution rate  
- Avoid overheating heat‑sensitive ingredients  
- Stirring or shaking improves dissolution

---

## Aqueous Solutions

- **Water** is the primary solvent  
- Most common type of compounded solution  
- Nonaqueous solutions use solvents other than water or contain minimal water

### Syrups (Enteral)

Sweet, viscous **aqueous** solutions saturated with **sucrose** or other sugars.

**Characteristics**:

- Sweetened and flavored  
- Contain **<10% alcohol**  
- May be prepared **with or without heat**

**Preparation Notes** 🛡️

- Heating speeds dissolution but may cause:
  - Sucrose darkening  
  - Caramelization  
  - Fermentation  
- Without heat: stir or shake in a vessel **twice the final volume**

**Sweetening Agents**:

- Sugars: sucrose, glucose, fructose  
- Polyols: sorbitol, glycerin, propylene glycol, mannitol  
- Nonnutritive sweeteners: aspartame, saccharin  
  - ⚠️ Do **not** provide viscosity → add **methylcellulose** as thickener

---

## Glycerates (Sugar-Based) Solutions

Solutions containing **glycerin** as the primary solvent.  
Used for drugs requiring a **viscous, stable** vehicle.

---

## Alcohol-Based

### Elixirs (Enteral)

Clear, sweetened **hydroalcoholic** solutions.

**Characteristics**:

- Dissolve both **alcohol‑soluble** and **water‑soluble** drugs  
- Less viscous and less sweet than syrups  
- Alcohol content: **5–40%**

---

### Collodions (Dermal)

- Alcohol/ether‑based  
- Form a **thin, flexible film** upon evaporation  
- Used for protective coatings or wart treatments  

---

### Spirits / Essences (Enteral)

- Alcoholic or hydroalcoholic solutions of **volatile substances** (e.g., essential oils)  
- Alcohol content: **62–85%**  
- Used as **flavoring agents** or for mild medicinal effects  

---

### Tinctures

#### Enteral Tinctures

- Alcoholic or hydroalcoholic solutions of **nonvolatile substances**  
- **10% strength** for potent drugs  
- **20% strength** for non‑potent drugs  

#### Dermal / Parenteral Tinctures

- Small‑volume alcohol or water‑based preparations  
- Often used as **anti‑infectives**  
- Dispensed in **dropper bottles**

---

### Fluid Extracts

Highly concentrated **alcoholic** solutions of plant materials.  
Typically **1 mL extract = 1 g crude drug**.

---

## Oleaginous (Oil-Based) Solutions

Used when drugs are **lipophilic** or require slow absorption.

Common vehicles:

- Corn oil  
- Peanut oil  
- Mineral oil  

May be **enteral** or **parenteral** depending on formulation.

---

## Liniments (Topical / Dermal)

- Alcoholic or oleaginous solutions  
- Applied with **friction**  
- Used for **counterirritation**, warming, or soothing effects
