# Topicals

Topical dosage forms are applied to the **skin or mucous membranes** for **local** or **systemic** effects.  

They may be **solutions, suspensions, emulsions, semisolids, or ointment bases** depending on therapeutic need.

---

## Lotions

Fluid, low‑viscosity **liquid topicals** used for:

- Lubrication  
- Cooling  
- Covering large or hairy areas  

Lotions may be:

- **Suspensions** (shake well)  
- **Emulsions** (o/w or w/o)

📌 *Lotions spread easily and dry quickly.*

---

## Creams

Creams are **emulsions** that exist as **very thick liquids or soft solids**.

Used for:

- **Emollient** effects (softening/smoothing skin)  
- Moisturizing  
- Delivering active drugs with good spreadability  

Characteristics:

- Easy to apply  
- Easy to remove  
- Less greasy than ointments  
- Can be **o/w** (most common) or **w/o**

📌 *Creams = emulsions with a soft, spreadable consistency.*

---

## Ointments

Ointments are **semisolid preparations**, often emulsions containing a **large proportion of base** with or without dispersed solids.

Used for:

- **Protectants**  
- **Emollients**  
- **Antiseptics**  
- **Antipruritics**  
- **Drug delivery** (active drug mixed into base)

Ointments vary widely depending on the **base type**

| Ointment Base Type | Water Content | Spreadability | Washability | Greasiness | Occlusive? | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Oleaginous (Hydrocarbon)** | Anhydrous | Difficult | Non‑washable | Greasy | Yes | White Petrolatum USP, White Ointment USP |
| **Absorption Bases (Anhydrous)** | Anhydrous | Difficult | Non‑washable | Greasy | Yes | Hydrophilic Petrolatum USP, Aquaphor |
| **Absorption Bases (W/O Emulsion)** | Hydrous | Moderate to Easy | Poorly washable | Greasy | Sometimes | Hydrous Lanolin, Eucerin, Nivea |
| **O/W Emulsion Bases (Cream Bases)** | Hydrous | Easy | Washable | Non‑greasy | No | Hydrophilic Ointment USP, Versabase |
| **Water‑Miscible Bases** | Hydrous | Moderate to Easy | Washable | Non‑greasy | No | PEG Ointment USP, Polybase |

- **More oil = more occlusive, more greasy, less washable; less drug penetration**  
- **More water = easier to spread, easier to wash off; more drug penetration**  
- **O/W = cream‑like**  
- **W/O = ointment‑like**

📌 *Occlusive bases trap moisture → good for dry, cracked skin.*

---

## Compounding

Topical compounding involves incorporating active ingredients into a **semisolid base** using techniques that ensure:

- Uniformity  
- Smooth texture  
- Stability  
- Appropriate consistency for the intended route  

Two major approaches are used depending on whether the **base** or the **drug** is manipulated first.

| Method | Best For | Heat? | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Base‑First** | Powders, pastes, mixing two ointments | No | Fastest; uses spatulation |
| **Fusion (Base‑Second)** | Creams, lotions, emulsions, wax‑containing ointments | Yes | Requires phase control and slow cooling |

---

### 🧴 Base‑First Method

`For mixing two ointments OR adding drug powder to an existing base`

Used when the **base is already prepared** (commercial or pre‑made) and the drug is incorporated by **spatulation**.

**Steps**:

1. Place the base on an **ointment slab** or parchment pad.  
2. Add the drug (powder or second ointment) on top.  
3. 🔗 Use **[Spatulation](./solids.md#spatulation)** to mix until uniform.  
4. Transfer the finished ointment into a jar **just large enough** for the final volume.  
5. Use the spatula to **press out air pockets** (“bleeding the jar”).  
6. Wipe excess product from:
   - Jar rim  
   - Threads  
   - Outside of container  
7. Cap tightly and label.

📌 *Base‑first = no heating required; ideal for powders, pastes, and simple mixtures.*

---

### 🧪 Base‑Second (Fusion) Method

`For ointments, creams, lotions, and emulsified semisolids`

Used when ingredients must be **melted**, **combined**, or **emulsified** before forming the final product.

This method relies on **controlled heating**, **phase separation**, and **careful cooling**

#### Phase Preparation

1. **Oleaginous (Oil) Phase**: Heat oil‑soluble ingredients in a beaker to **~70°C**.
2. **Aqueous (Water) Phase**: Heat water‑soluble ingredients in a separate beaker to **a few degrees higher** than the oil phase.

📌 *Water cools faster than oil → heat slightly higher to match temperatures during mixing.*

##### Melting Order

`Melt highest melting point → lowest melting point`

This minimizes thermal degradation and ensures uniform melting.

| Ingredient | Melting Point (°C) |
| --- | --- |
| Carnauba Wax | 81–86 |
| Cetyl Alcohol | 45–50 |
| Cetyl Esters Wax | 43–47 |
| Cholesterol | 147–150 |
| Cocoa Butter | 30–35 |
| Glyceryl Monostearate | ~55 |
| Stearic Acid | 69–70 |
| Stearyl Alcohol | 55–60 |
| White Wax / Beeswax | 62–65 |
| PEG 1500 | 44–48 |
| PEG 3350 | 54–58 |
| PEG 6000 | 58–63 |

🛡️ *Use the **lowest temperature** and **shortest heating time** possible to reduce water loss and maintain stability.*

#### Combining Phases

1. Remove both beakers from heat.  
2. **Slowly add the internal phase to the external phase** with continuous stirring.  
   - O/W emulsion → add **oil → water**  
   - W/O emulsion → add **water → oil**  
3. Stir continuously until the mixture begins to **congeal**.

> 📌 *Adding internal → external prevents phase inversion.*

#### Cooling Step

Cooling determines the **final texture** and **stability**.

- **Do NOT accelerate cooling** (no ice baths, no refrigeration).  
  - Rapid cooling causes graininess, separation, or brittleness.  
- Add **volatile ingredients** (menthol, essential oils, fragrances) **only after cooling**.  
- Cool until the product is **just above solidification temperature**, then pour into jars or tubes.

📌 *Pouring too hot causes layering; pouring too cold causes uneven texture.*
