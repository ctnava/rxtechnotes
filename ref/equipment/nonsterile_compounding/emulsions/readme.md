# Emulsions

An **emulsion** is an *unstable, two‑phase* system consisting of **two immiscible liquids**, where one liquid is **dispersed as droplets** within the other and **stabilized by an emulsifier**.

> 📌 Without an emulsifier, the two liquids will separate into distinct phases (i.e. layers).

---

## 🔑 Definitions

### Immiscible

Liquids that **do not mix** or dissolve in each other (e.g., oil + water).

### Emulsifying Agents

`Emulsifiers, Surfactants, or Surface Active Agents`

A substance that **prevents separation** of the two immiscible phases by:

- Providing a **protective barrier** around droplets  
- Reducing **interfacial tension**  
- Preventing droplets from coalescing  
- Allowing temporary separation that can be corrected by shaking  
- Preventing **breaking**, which is irreversible and cannot be redispersed

Common emulsifiers:

- **Sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS)**  
- **Sodium dioctyl sulfosuccinate**  
- **Spans** (lipophilic sorbitan esters)  
- **Tweens** (hydrophilic polysorbates)  
- **Pluronics** (block copolymers)

📌 *Emulsifiers determine the type of emulsion formed.*

### Separation (Creaming / Sedimentation)

- Droplets rise (creaming) or fall (sedimentation)  
- **Reversible** with shaking  
- Acceptable in many emulsions

### Coalescence

- Droplets merge into larger droplets  
- Leads to instability  
- **Partially reversible**

### Breaking (Cracking)

- Complete separation into two layers  
- **Irreversible**  
- Cannot be fixed by shaking  
- Emulsion must be discarded

### Phase Inversion

- Emulsion flips from **o/w → w/o** or vice versa  
- Caused by:
  - Temperature changes  
  - Incorrect emulsifier ratio  
  - Excessive dilution with the wrong phase  

🛡️ *Avoid adding the external phase too quickly; add the internal phase slowly with constant mixing.*

---

### Types of Emulsions

#### Oil‑in‑Water (o/w)

- **Aqueous phase > 45%**  
- Oil droplets dispersed in water  
- Requires **hydrophilic emulsifier**  
- Feels **non‑greasy**, washes off easily  
- Common for:
  - Oral emulsions  
  - Injectable emulsions  
  - Lotions and creams  

#### Water‑in‑Oil (w/o)

- **Aqueous phase < 45%**  
- Water droplets dispersed in oil  
- Requires **lipophilic emulsifier**  
- Feels **greasy**, occlusive  
- Common for:
  - Protective ointments  
  - Moisturizing creams  
  - Topical formulations needing barrier effects

## Preparation Methods

All emulsion‑making methods require **energy** in the form of:

- **Shaking**
- **Trituration / levigation**
- **Heat**
- **Mechanical stirring**

The goal is to create a **stable primary emulsion** before adding additional ingredients.

| Method | Ratio | When Used | Key Feature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Continental (Dry Gum)** | 4:2:1 (Oil:Water:Gum) | Most fixed/volatile oils | Water added **all at once** |
| **Wet Gum** | 1:2:4 (Gum:Water:Oil) | Difficult oils; controlled hydration | Oil added **slowly** |
| **Beaker** | N/A | Creams, lotions, semisolids | Heat both phases to **70°C** |

### 🥣 Continental (Dry Gum) Method

`4 : 2 : 1 - Oil : Water : Gum`

Used for: **O/W emulsions** with **volatile oils** or **fixed oils**.  
Produces a **quick, stable primary emulsion**.

**Steps**:

1. Use a **wedgewood or porcelain mortar** (never glass; too smooth).  
2. Levigate **1 part gum** with **4 parts oil** until smooth.  
3. Add **2 parts water all at once** and triturate vigorously.  
4. A **primary emulsion** forms in **1–2 minutes**, indicated by:
   - A **creamy white** appearance  
   - A characteristic **“cracking” sound**  
5. Add additional ingredients **after** the primary emulsion is formed.

> 📌 *Dry gum = water added all at once.*

### 💧 Wet Gum Method

`1 : 2 : 4 — Gum : Water : Oil`

Used when:

- Gum must be **fully hydrated first**  
- Oils are **difficult to emulsify**  
- A more **controlled** process is needed

**Steps**:

1. Levigate **1 part gum** with **2 parts water** to form a **mucilage**.  
2. Slowly add **4 parts oil** in small portions while triturating.  
3. After all oil is incorporated, continue trituration for several minutes to form the **primary emulsion**.  
4. Add remaining ingredients as needed.

📌 *Wet gum = oil added slowly.*

### 🧪 Beaker Method

`For semisolids: creams, lotions, ointment‑like emulsions`

Used when both phases contain **multiple ingredients** that must be melted or dissolved separately.

**Steps**:

1. Divide ingredients into:
   - **Oil‑soluble components**  
   - **Water‑soluble components**
2. Heat both beakers to **~70°C** (same temperature prevents premature solidification).  
3. Remove from heat.  
4. **Slowly add the internal phase to the external phase** with continuous stirring.  
   - Prevents **phase inversion**  
5. Continue stirring while cooling to **room temperature** using:
   - Stir rod  
   - Spatula  
   - Magnetic stir bar  

📌 *Beaker method = heat both phases → combine → cool with stirring.*

#### 🔑 Internal vs External Phases

- **Internal phase (dispersed phase)**  
  The phase that becomes **droplets** inside the emulsion.  
  - In **o/w emulsions**, the internal phase = **oil**  
  - In **w/o emulsions**, the internal phase = **water**

- **External phase (continuous phase)**  
  The phase that forms the **surrounding medium**.  
  - In **o/w emulsions**, the external phase = **water**  
  - In **w/o emulsions**, the external phase = **oil**

📌 *The external phase determines the “feel” of the product (greasy vs nongreasy).*

📌 *During preparation, the **internal phase is always added to the external phase**, never the reverse.*
