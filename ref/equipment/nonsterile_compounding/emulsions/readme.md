# Emulsions

An **emulsion** is an *unstable, two‑phase* system consisting of **two immiscible liquids**, where one liquid is **dispersed as droplets** within the other and **stabilized by an emulsifier**.

> 📌 Without an emulsifier, the two liquids will separate into distinct phases (i.e. layers).

---

## 🔑 Definitions

### Immiscible

Liquids that **do not mix** or dissolve in each other (e.g., oil + water).

### Emulsifier (Emulsifying Agent)

A substance that **prevents separation** of the two immiscible phases by:

- Providing a **protective barrier** around droplets  
- Reducing **interfacial tension**  
- Preventing droplets from coalescing  
- Allowing temporary separation that can be corrected by shaking  
- Preventing **breaking**, which is irreversible and cannot be redispersed

Common emulsifiers:

- **Sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS)**  
- **Sodium dioctyl sulfosuccinate**  
- **Spans** (lipophilic sorbitan esters)  
- **Tweens** (hydrophilic polysorbates)  
- **Pluronics** (block copolymers)

📌 *Emulsifiers determine the type of emulsion formed.*

### Separation (Creaming / Sedimentation)

- Droplets rise (creaming) or fall (sedimentation)  
- **Reversible** with shaking  
- Acceptable in many emulsions

### Coalescence

- Droplets merge into larger droplets  
- Leads to instability  
- **Partially reversible**

### Breaking (Cracking)

- Complete separation into two layers  
- **Irreversible**  
- Cannot be fixed by shaking  
- Emulsion must be discarded

### Phase Inversion

- Emulsion flips from **o/w → w/o** or vice versa  
- Caused by:
  - Temperature changes  
  - Incorrect emulsifier ratio  
  - Excessive dilution with the wrong phase  

🛡️ *Avoid adding the external phase too quickly; add the internal phase slowly with constant mixing.*

---

### Types of Emulsions

#### Oil‑in‑Water (o/w)

- **Aqueous phase > 45%**  
- Oil droplets dispersed in water  
- Requires **hydrophilic emulsifier**  
- Feels **non‑greasy**, washes off easily  
- Common for:
  - Oral emulsions  
  - Injectable emulsions  
  - Lotions and creams  

#### Water‑in‑Oil (w/o)

- **Aqueous phase < 45%**  
- Water droplets dispersed in oil  
- Requires **lipophilic emulsifier**  
- Feels **greasy**, occlusive  
- Common for:
  - Protective ointments  
  - Moisturizing creams  
  - Topical formulations needing barrier effects
