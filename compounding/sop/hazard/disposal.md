# Hazardous Materials Disposal

## Resource Conservation & Recovery Act (RCRA, 1976)

`Enforced by the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)`

RCRA governs how pharmacies identify, store, track, and dispose of **hazardous waste**, including certain pharmaceuticals that pose environmental or public‑health risks if discarded improperly.

🔑 **Key Points for Pharmacies**

- Establishes rules for **hazardous waste generation**, labeling, storage, and disposal.
- Prohibits the **sewering** (flushing or drain disposal) of hazardous waste pharmaceuticals.
- Requires proper segregation of:
  - **Hazardous waste pharmaceuticals** (e.g., P‑listed, U‑listed)
  - **Non‑hazardous pharmaceutical waste**
  - **Controlled substances** (handled separately under DEA rules)
- Defines generator categories (e.g., VSQG, SQG) based on the amount of hazardous waste produced.

`The EPA's definition of hazardous exclusively pertains to the environment.`

📖 **EPA Healthcare-Specific Rule (2019)**

The EPA's *Management Standards for Hazardous Waste Pharmaceuticals* created streamlined requirements for healthcare facilities, including pharmacies:

- Simplifies hazardous waste handling in healthcare settings.
- Removes **nicotine** replacement therapies (patches, gums, lozenges) from the P075 hazardous waste listing.
- Requires facilities to follow **no‑sewering** rules for all hazardous waste pharmaceuticals.
- Provides clearer standards for long‑term care, retail, and hospital pharmacies.

> ⚠️ RCRA rules apply to **waste**, not to active inventory. A drug becomes RCRA‑regulated only when it is discarded.

💊 **Examples of RCRA Hazardous Waste Pharmaceuticals**

- **P‑listed (Immediately Dangerous)**: warfarin (unused), arsenic trioxide  
- **U‑listed (Toxic; Dangerous Long-Term)**: cyclophosphamide, chloral hydrate  
- **Characteristic Waste**: ignitable alcohol formulations, certain aerosols
  - **Ignitable**: flash point < 60°C / 140°F
  - **Corrosive**
  - **Reactive**
  - **Toxic**: fails the Toxicity Characteristic Leaching Procedure (TCLP, EPA Method 1311) test

> ⚠️ Non-listed (U/P) HDs fall under Characteristic Waste.

## Waste Segregation & Disposal Systems

Waste disposal in healthcare is governed by environmental, occupational, and public health regulations. Improper disposal can harm workers, contaminate groundwater, or violate federal law. Pharmacies use color‑coded waste streams to ensure compliance.

This distinction prevents environmental contamination and ensures compliance with regulations.

### 🟥 Red Bins — Infectious & Biohazardous Waste

Red bags and red sharps containers are used for materials contaminated with **blood, bodily fluids, or infectious agents**.

Examples include:

- **SHARPS**: items that may cut or puncture (Used needles, lancets, and syringes)
- Blood‑soaked gauze or dressings  
- Contaminated gloves or PPE  

Reusable items such as scrubs or linens are **sanitized**, not discarded.

### ⬛ Black Bins — Hazardous Pharmaceutical Waste (Bulk RCRA Waste)

Black bins are reserved for full containers of medications classified as hazardous by **NIOSH**, **EPA**, **DEA**, or **DOT**. These substances pose risks such as carcinogenicity, reproductive toxicity, organ damage, or environmental contamination.

Licensed handlers in permitted facilities must segregate, manifest, collect, and incinerate RCRA waste at high temperatures. These must never fall into sewers or wastewater.

The definition of "bulk" waste applies to:

- U/P-Listed drug containers or vials (full or not)
- Materials with trace amount of U/P-Listed drugs
- HD drug containers or vials (full)
- Spill Cleanup Supplies

### 🟨 Yellow Bins — Trace Chemotherapy & Cytotoxic Waste (Empty Waste)

Yellow bins are used for **trace chemotherapy waste**, meaning items contaminated with small amounts of cytotoxic drugs.

Examples include:

- Disposable gowns and gloves used during chemo handling  
- Empty IV bags, tubing, and vials  
- Wipes and absorbent pads used during compounding  

Empty waste must be separated and incinerated at a lower temperature.

To meet the RCRA's criteria for "empty" waste:

- must be non-listed (U/P)
- contain less than 3% of the original container's contents
