# 💊 Medication Safety  

Medication safety focuses on preventing **Adverse Drug Events (ADEs)** and reducing the risk of **medication errors**, two of the most important responsibilities in any pharmacy setting.

---

## ⚠️ Adverse Drug Events (ADEs)

An **Adverse Drug Event (ADE)** is any injury or harm resulting from medication use.  
Severity can range from mild to life‑threatening, including:

- Allergic reactions  
- Organ toxicity (e.g., kidney failure)  
- Life‑threatening complications or death  

> 🩺 *Not all ADEs are caused by errors. Some are unavoidable due to patient‑specific factors.*

Many ADEs **can** be prevented through proper monitoring, communication, and adherence to safety protocols.

---

## 🚨 Medication Errors  

Medication errors are a **major preventable cause** of ADEs.  
They can occur at **any stage** of the medication‑use process: prescribing, transcribing, dispensing, compounding, administration, or monitoring.

> 🤧 *Age‑related errors are especially common: older adults often take many medications, and pediatric patients are highly vulnerable to dosing mistakes.*

### Common Causes of Medication Errors

- Distraction during the fill process (e.g., answering phones)  
- Incorrect IV pump programming  
- Automated Dispensing Cabinet (ADC) stocked incorrectly  
- Computerized Prescriber Order Entry (CPOE) mistakes  
- Decimal point errors  
- Delays in order processing or dispensing  
- Unclear or incomplete directions  
- Inadequate drug monitoring  
- Illegible handwriting  
- IV access issues  
- Look‑Alike / Sound‑Alike (LASA) medications  
- Miscalculations  
- Miscommunication between staff  
- Over‑reliance on technology  
- Patient identification failure  
- Policies or procedures not followed  
- Trailing zeros (e.g., “1.0 mg” → misread as 10 mg)  
- Verbal orders misinterpreted  
- Workflow interruptions  

> 🛡️ *Most errors are system‑based, not individual failures.*

### 🧭 Medication Error Types

Medication errors can occur at any point in the medication‑use process.  
This table summarizes **common error types**, **what they mean**, and **their potential consequences**.

| Error Type | Definition | Example | Possible Consequences |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Administration Error** | Medication is given incorrectly (wrong rate, route, technique, or timing) | IV medication infused too quickly | Overdose, toxicity, cardiac/respiratory distress |
| **Allergy Error** | Medication given despite a documented allergy | Prescriber orders penicillin for a penicillin‑allergic patient | Rash, anaphylaxis, airway compromise |
| **Compounding Error** | Incorrect preparation of a sterile or non‑sterile product | Decimal error → dose compounded 10× too strong | Toxicity, treatment failure, severe ADE |
| **Dispensing Error** | Wrong medication, strength, or product dispensed | Metformin dispensed instead of metronidazole | Uncontrolled disease, hypoglycemia, ADE |
| **Duplicate Therapy** | Patient receives two drugs from the same class unnecessarily | Two prescribers issue similar antihypertensives | Hypotension, bradycardia, organ damage |
| **Expired Product** | Medication used past expiration or BUD | Expired IV bag delivered to nursing unit | Reduced potency, contamination risk |
| **Extra Dose** | More doses given than prescribed | MAR not updated → patient receives dose twice | Toxicity, overdose, organ injury |
| **Incorrect Dosage Form** | Wrong formulation dispensed | Otic drops dispensed instead of ophthalmic | Eye injury, treatment failure |
| **Missed Dose** | Scheduled dose not administered | Nurse forgets medication and no follow‑up occurs | Disease progression, hospitalization, death |
| **Omission Error** | Required medication not ordered or continued | Maintenance meds not included in admission orders | Withdrawal, decompensation, relapse |
| **Prescribing Error** | Incorrect drug, dose, frequency, or route ordered | Dose far outside safe range | Toxicity, subtherapeutic treatment |
| **Wrong Patient** | Medication intended for one patient given to another | Label applied to wrong bag | Severe ADE, allergic reaction, death |

> 🛡️ *Most medication errors are preventable with proper workflow, communication, and verification.*
