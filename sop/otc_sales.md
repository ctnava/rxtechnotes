# SOP: Sale of OTC Listed Chemical Substances (CMEA Compliance)

## 1. Purpose

To establish standardized procedures for the sale, documentation, storage, and reporting of **OTC Listed Chemical Substances** regulated under the **Combat Methamphetamine Epidemic Act (CMEA, 2005)**, including pseudoephedrine (PSE), ephedrine, and phenylpropanolamine. This SOP ensures compliance with federal and state law, prevents diversion, and protects patient safety.

## 2. Scope

This SOP applies to all pharmacy personnel involved in:

- Selling OTC listed chemical products  
- Verifying customer identity and eligibility  
- Maintaining logbooks and electronic tracking systems  
- Securing listed chemical inventory  
- Ensuring compliance with quantity limits and age restrictions  

## 3. Definitions

**Listed Chemical Substances (CMEA):**  
OTC products containing pseudoephedrine, ephedrine, or phenylpropanolamine that may be used in the illicit manufacture of methamphetamine.

**Behind-the-Counter (BTC):**  
OTC Products must be stored in a location inaccessible to customers and dispensed directly by pharmacy staff.

**Logbook:**  
A written or electronic record documenting sales of listed chemical products exceeding 60 mg of pseudoephedrine base.

## 4. Responsibilities

### 4.1 Pharmacy Technicians

- Verify customer age and identification  
- Check purchase limits and logbook entries  
- Retrieve BTC products and complete the sale  
- Maintain secure storage of listed chemicals  
- Document all required information accurately  
- Notify the pharmacist of suspicious behavior or attempted excessive purchases  

### 4.2 Pharmacists

- Oversee compliance with CMEA requirements  
- Review logbook entries as needed  
- Handle escalations involving suspicious activity  
- Ensure proper training of pharmacy staff  

### 4.3 Pharmacy Manager / PIC

- Maintain logbooks and electronic tracking systems  
- Ensure secure storage and inventory controls  
- Conduct periodic audits for compliance  
- Maintain staff training records  

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Product Storage & Access

- Listed chemical products must be stored:
  - Behind the pharmacy counter **or**
  - In a locked case requiring staff assistance  
- Customers may not access these products directly.  
- Only trained pharmacy personnel may retrieve and dispense these items.

### 5.2 Customer Eligibility Verification

Before completing a sale:

1. Confirm the purchaser is **18 years or older**.  
2. Request a **valid, government-issued photo ID**.  
3. Verify the name on the ID matches the logbook entry.  
4. Ensure the purchaser has not exceeded legal limits.

### 5.3 Quantity Limits (CMEA)

| Time Period | Maximum Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Per Day** | **3.6 grams** | Applies to all combined purchases |
| **30 Days (in-person)** | **9 grams** | Retail pharmacy limit |
| **30 Days (mail-order)** | **7.5 grams** | Stricter limit for online/mobile vendors |

### 5.4 Logbook Requirements

A logbook entry is required for any sale involving **more than 60 mg** of pseudoephedrine base.

The logbook must include:

- Purchaser’s **name**  
- Purchaser’s **address**  
- Purchaser’s **signature**  
- Date and time of sale  
- Product name  
- Quantity sold  
- (only by protocol) Staff initials  

Logbooks must be:

- Maintained for **2 years** (federal)  
- Readily retrievable for inspection  
- Stored securely to protect customer information  

### 5.5 Completing the Sale

1. Retrieve the product from BTC storage.  
2. Verify ID and age.  
3. Check purchase limits in the logbook or electronic tracking system.  
4. Record all required information.  
5. Have the customer sign the logbook (if applicable).  
6. Complete the sale and provide the product directly to the customer.  

### 5.6 Refusing a Sale

A sale must be refused if:

- The customer exceeds quantity limits  
- The customer refuses to show ID  
- The customer is under 18  
- The customer refuses to sign the logbook  
- The customer exhibits suspicious behavior (e.g., repeated attempts, nervousness, evasiveness)  

Document the refusal and notify the pharmacist.

### 5.7 Inventory Management

- Listed chemicals must be inventoried regularly.  
- Discrepancies must be reported to the pharmacist immediately.  
- Theft or significant loss must be reported using:
  - **DEA Form 106** within one business day  
  - State board notification as required  

## 6. Training Requirements

All pharmacy staff must receive annual training on:

- CMEA regulations  
- Logbook procedures  
- ID verification  
- Quantity limits  
- Suspicious activity recognition  

Training must be documented and retained per facility policy.

## 7. References

- Combat Methamphetamine Epidemic Act (CMEA, 2005)  
- 21 USC §830 – Listed Chemicals  
- 21 CFR §1314 – Retail Sales of Scheduled Listed Chemical Products  
- DEA Diversion Control Division
