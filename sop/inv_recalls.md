# Handling Drug and Product Recalls

## 1. Purpose

To establish a standardized procedure for identifying, processing, documenting, and responding to drug and product recalls issued by the FDA, manufacturers, or wholesalers.

## 2. Scope

This SOP applies to all pharmacy personnel involved in inventory management, dispensing, patient safety, and regulatory compliance.

## 3. Definitions

**MedWatch:** FDA’s adverse event and product problem reporting system.

**VAERS:** Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System.

**Recall:** Removal or correction of a marketed product that violates FDA laws or poses a safety risk.

### Recall Classes

- **Class I:** Reasonable probability of serious adverse health consequences or death.
- **Class II:** Temporary or medically reversible adverse effects.
- **Class III:** Not likely to cause adverse health consequences.

## 4. Responsibilities

### 4.1 Pharmacy Technicians

- Monitor recall notices from FDA, wholesalers, and manufacturers.
- Immediately notify the pharmacist and store management.
- Identify affected inventory.
- Quarantine recalled products.
- Assist with patient outreach lists and arrange for refunds & exchanges
- Document actions taken.

### 4.2 Pharmacists

- Verify recall classification and instructions.
- Authorize removal and quarantine of affected products.
- Oversee patient and provider notifications.
- Assess clinical risk.
- Ensure proper return, disposal, or remediation.
- Finalize documentation.

### 4.3 Pharmacy Manager / PIC

- Ensure compliance with recall procedures.
- Maintain recall logs.
- Conduct periodic audits.

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Recall Identification

Recalls may be identified through:

- FDA MedWatch alerts  
- FDA Weekly Enforcement Report  
- Manufacturer or wholesaler notifications  
- VAERS (for vaccines)  
- Internal reports of product defects  

Upon receiving a recall notice:

1. Technician forwards the notice to the pharmacist.
2. Pharmacist verifies recall class, affected lots, and instructions.

### 5.2 Verification of Affected Inventory

1. Technician retrieves recall details:
   - Product name  
   - Strength and dosage form  
   - NDC  
   - Lot number(s) & Batch(es)
   - Expiration date(s)  

2. Compare recall details with:
   - On‑shelf inventory  
   - Backstock  
   - Automated dispensing cabinets  
   - Patient‑specific bins  

3. Document whether affected lots are present.

### 5.3 Removal and Quarantine

1. Remove all affected products from shelves and storage.
2. Place items in a clearly labeled quarantine bin:
   - “RECALLED — DO NOT DISPENSE”
3. Prevent further distribution.

### 5.4 Patient and Provider Notification

#### Technician Responsibilities

- Generate list of affected patients.
- Prepare call lists, letters, or portal messages.

#### Pharmacist Responsibilities

- Contact patients to discontinue use.
- Provide replacement or refund.
- Assess for adverse effects.
- Notify prescribers when necessary.

### 5.5 Follow Manufacturer or FDA Instructions

Instructions may include:

- Returning product to wholesaler  
- Destroying product per hazardous waste rules  
- Completing recall response forms  
- Tracking recall effectiveness  

Technicians assist; pharmacists verify compliance.

### 5.6 Documentation

Record the following in the Recall Log:

- Date recall was received  
- Product details (NDC, lot, expiration)  
- Recall class  
- Quantity removed  
- Patient notifications completed  
- Return/disposal method  
- Staff initials and pharmacist verification  

Retention: Minimum 3 years or per state law.

## 6. Technician Quick‑Reference Workflow

| Step     | Description                                         |
|----------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| Verify   | Confirm recall details and check inventory.         |
| Remove   | Pull affected products and quarantine them.         |
| Notify   | Assist with patient/provider outreach.              |
| Follow   | Carry out manufacturer/FDA instructions.            |
| Document | Log all actions for compliance.                     |
